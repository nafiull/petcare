Pet Food Safety

Know what's safe, what's risky, what's off-limits.

A quick-reference guide to common foods. Always introduce new foods gradually and consult your vet if unsure.

Generally Safe

Plainly prepared, in moderation
  • Plain cooked chicken (no bones or seasoning)
  • Carrots, cut into safe pieces
  • Blueberries (a few at a time)
  • Plain cooked pumpkin
  • Apple slices (no seeds or core)

Use Caution

Small amounts, vet guidance
  • Cheese and dairy (lactose sensitivity)
  • Peanut butter (check for xylitol)
  • Cooked eggs (some dogs sensitive)
  • Cooked boneless salmon
  • Bananas (high sugar, occasional)

Dangerous or Toxic

Do not feed
  • Chocolate — theobromine toxicity
  • Grapes & raisins — kidney failure risk
  • Xylitol — liver failure, hypoglycemia
  • Onions, garlic, chives — damage red blood cells
  • Macadamia nuts, alcohol, yeast dough

⚠️ Note: Not a complete list. Even "safe" foods should be plain, unseasoned, and given in moderation. If you suspect poisoning, contact your vet or the ASPCA Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435.

Appropriate

Complete and balanced
  • Complete and balanced cat food (wet or dry)
  • Plain cooked chicken, small portions
  • Plain cooked turkey, boneless
  • Commercial cat-formulated treats

Use Caution

Occasional, small amounts
  • Cheese (most cats are lactose intolerant)
  • Cooked eggs (occasional)
  • Lean cooked beef, small amounts
  • Plain cooked pumpkin

Dangerous or Toxic

Do not feed
  • Onions, garlic, chives — damage red blood cells
  • Chocolate, alcohol, caffeine
  • Grapes & raisins, xylitol
  • Raw fish — parasites, thiamine loss
  • Dog food — not feline-formulated

⚠️ Note: Not a complete list. If you suspect poisoning, contact your vet or the ASPCA Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435.
